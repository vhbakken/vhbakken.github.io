Data Analytics Project
Stocks, cryptocurrencies, and commodities; what do they all have in common? They can all be analyzed to extract valuable insights. As part of my studies in Hong Kong, I took the course Data Analytics with Excel VBA at the City University of Hong Kong, where our group project involved building a program to analyze various financial assets.
Project Details
As part of a group project at the City University of Hong Kong, we developed a fully automated Excel VBA application for analyzing cryptocurrency options. The tool targets a core challenge in options trading: determining whether options are expensive or cheap relative to recent price movement. It does this by computing Implied Volatility (IV) and Realized Volatility (RV) and comparing the two. Rather than pulling data manually across separate spreadsheets, the application accepts raw CSV exports from Deribit and Binance, processes everything in a single run, and auto-generates structured output workbooks with tables and charts.
Under the hood, the application uses a Newton-Raphson solver to compute implied volatility row-by-row for each option contract, and calculates realized volatility across seven rolling time windows ranging from 7 to 180 days using annualized log returns. Results are summarized into volatility spread signals, Rich, Fair, or Cheap, alongside a full Greeks breakdown and an optional 3D volatility surface. The project replaced a slow and error-prone manual workflow with a fast and consistent single-click reporting tool built entirely within Excel VBA.
